## Product case and need evidence

**For a Berlin resident, when a nearby construction site raises questions about what is happening and when it should finish, this helps them explain the source-stated plan and its uncertainty by moving from a place to a dated record and its evidence, compared with searching separate official pages.** The hiring manager is a separate audience assessing Gregory's product and implementation judgment.

- **Hypothesis:** use is occasional and triggered by a place, disruption or changed announcement. Stakes are understanding local plans and avoiding false confidence, not safety-critical route planning.
- **Observed product behavior, 2026-09-22:** the Europaplatz dossier retains earlier statements, a current recorded year, an unresolved funding conflict and withheld conclusions. This demonstrates traceability, not resident demand.
- **External evidence, directly inspected 2026-09-22:** the [official Europaplatz page](https://www.berlin.de/sen/bauen/baukultur/berliner-plaetzeprogramm/europaplatz-sued-1567894.php) displays “Fertigstellung: 2026” and “Laufende Maßnahme.” For someone who only needs that year, the official page may already suffice. This review did not independently validate historical interpretations or observe actual progress.
- **Owner/project record:** README and the accepted ADRs describe an evidence-first civic product and disclose the author's German-language limitation. These are design rationale, not interviews or market validation.

The proposed switching advantage is geographic discovery plus a preserved evidence history. A document or official page can handle a single known site; an atlas earns its complexity only if people need to identify places or compare changing records. A general chatbot adds no inherent authority. With just three full dossiers and 150 basic register listings, most visitors cannot yet expect a full answer about their own site. No time savings, adoption, accuracy or willingness to return was measured.

## Smallest need-validation experiment and handoff

**Owner:** recruit three Berlin residents who can assess the German source, each with a real construction question. This review sends no invitations. Ask them first to find the official answer for a relevant reviewed site, then use the atlas, alternating order between participants. Observe whether they explain the date as a plan, identify its source/as-of boundary and state what remains unknown. Ask whether a real recent question made the history useful; do not lead with the feature list.

**Proposed thresholds, not results:** continue if at least two of three reach the supported conclusion without coaching within two minutes and can name a concrete use for the evidence history beyond the official page. Reconsider the map-first proposition if two cannot find a relevant record, prefer the official page with no lost context, or read a plan as confirmed completion. Three sessions can disprove obvious assumptions, not establish market demand.
