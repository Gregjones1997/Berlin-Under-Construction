# Design: A resident can verify what a Berlin construction date means

## Problem Statement

A Berlin resident or regular visitor sees a construction site and wants to know
what is changing, when an official source says it should finish, and how much
confidence to place in that statement. The current alternative is an official
project page, search results, or separate records that may omit earlier statements
and do not consistently distinguish a plan from observed completion.

The product case is plausible, but unvalidated. Working software and a successful
agent walkthrough establish functionality, not demand.

## The Six Forcing Questions

### 1. Demand reality

No resident interview, uncoached natural-user session, repeat-use observation,
adoption figure, or willingness-to-pay evidence was found. The strongest evidence
is that official sources publish dates in different contexts and the implemented
dossiers preserve changes, conflicts, and withheld conclusions. That establishes
an information-integration problem, not demand for this product.

**Status:** hypothesis.

### 2. Status quo

For a known project, a resident can search and read the official Berlin page. For
the reviewed Europaplatz example, that page already exposes “Fertigstellung: 2026.”
The atlas has to earn its extra complexity through place-based discovery, history,
precise evidence, and visible uncertainty. A chatbot has no inherent source
authority and a static document is sufficient when the place is already known and
only one current date matters.

### 3. Desperate specificity

The primary-user hypothesis is a Berlin resident or regular visitor asking about
a nearby site after seeing disruption or a changed announcement. The hiring manager
arriving from the portfolio is a separate demonstration audience. No named resident
or specific personal consequence has been observed, so frequency, urgency, and
return behavior remain hypotheses.

### 4. Narrowest useful product

One reviewed project journey is enough to test the proposition:

1. identify the place;
2. see the source-stated milestone and its as-of boundary;
3. understand that it is not observed progress;
4. open the exact German evidence and original source;
5. return to the same place with context preserved.

The existing C-014 Europaplatz dossier is that wedge. The other two dossiers and
150 basic listings provide orientation and breadth, but they must not imply equal
review depth.

### 5. Observation and surprise

No resident has been observed using the product without coaching. The agent review
found two usability surprises: mobile arrival hid the product promise, and the
standalone dossier put identity metadata ahead of the useful date. Those are expert
inspection findings, not user research.

### 6. Future fit

**Hypothesis:** as generated summaries become easier to produce, exact evidence,
source dates, preserved contradictions, and explicit withholding become more
valuable. This only strengthens the product if residents actually need the history
or discovery layer. It does not overcome the possibility that an official page is
good enough for most questions.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

For a Berlin resident, when a nearby construction site raises a question about what
is happening and when it should finish, the product helps them explain the
source-stated plan and its uncertainty by moving from a place to a dated record and
its evidence, compared with searching separate official pages.

The narrowest wedge is the prepared C-014 place-to-evidence journey, not an AI
assistant, account system, follow feature, or ten-project platform.

## Constraints

- German source wording is canonical; English only labels structure and controlled
  glossary concepts.
- No factual value publishes without an evidence span.
- Withheld values never enter the public projection.
- No natural person is named.
- No third-party runtime requests or map tiles.
- No new client island for the dossier experience.
- Current work is local; this workflow does not authorize deployment.
- The public release and the local candidate must be described separately.

## Premises

1. A supported date is useful only when its source and limitation are equally easy
   to inspect.
2. The atlas is justified only when place discovery or history matters; otherwise
   the official page is the simpler product.
3. Three reviewed dossiers plus clearly labelled basic listings are more honest than
   pretending to have uniform citywide coverage.
4. The present product and visual direction are established and should be refined,
   not replaced, unless observed resident behavior disproves the map-first entry.
5. Demand validation is now a human research task, not a software-completeness task.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Preserve the atlas and finish the evidence loop

Minimal viable approach. Keep the current City Desk atlas and three dossiers, make
the mobile purpose explicit, lead dossiers with the useful date, preserve place on
return, and document the prepared/live boundary.

- Effort: small
- Risk: low
- Reuses: current atlas, dossier renderer, static index, evidence disclosure, and
  contextual correction routes
- Tradeoff: breadth remains uneven and demand remains untested

### Approach B: Make a dossier-first evidence reader

Treat the atlas as secondary and lead every visit with a searchable evidence list or
single flagship dossier. This is simpler for a known project and may reduce map
friction.

- Effort: medium
- Risk: medium
- Reuses: static records and dossier components
- Tradeoff: abandons the established place-based portfolio direction before user
  evidence shows that it is wrong

### Approach C: Expand into monitoring and alerts

Add follows, change notifications, source-health monitoring, and broader coverage.
This could create repeat value if residents care about changes over time.

- Effort: large
- Risk: high
- Reuses: provenance and claim history
- Tradeoff: invents retention behavior before the one-session research job is
  validated and adds operational scope outside the current authorization

## Recommended Approach

Approach A. The owner explicitly directed the workflow to preserve established
product and visual direction, implement accepted revisions, and complete a coherent
end-to-end experience. The bounded fixes are already implemented locally and should
be verified, not redesigned. Approach B remains a falsifiable fallback if observed
residents cannot understand or use the map-first entry. Approach C is deferred.

## Current Implementation Status

The bounded Approach A changes are already present in local commit `f3bfe04`:

- the compact phone entry states the atlas purpose and links to the record index;
- the standalone dossier renders the current source-stated milestone before identity

They still require the independent `/qa`, `/design-review`, and `/review` passes in

## Open Questions

- Do residents have a recent construction question for which history or evidence
  improves on the official page?
- Can they interpret a source-stated date as a plan, not confirmed progress, without
  coaching?
- Does place-based discovery help them find a relevant record, or does it slow them
  down?
- Do native view-transition warnings reproduce outside the automated browser?

The warning refers to browser `InvalidStateError` messages emitted while native page
transitions run. Settled pages remained usable; attribution to the site or automation
host is unproven.

None of these questions blocks local verification. The first three block any claim
of demonstrated product demand.

## Success Criteria

For the implemented experience:

- At 390 px width, purpose and a route to a reviewed project are visible without
  guessing a map control.
- The C-014 first screen exposes the source-stated milestone, as-of boundary, and
  progress caveat.
- Keyboard use can open evidence and reach the official source.
- Returning to the atlas restores C-014 selection.
- A 503 response for the self-hosted `/atlas/model.json` manifest leaves an
  understandable “atlas could not load” state, retry control, and static-index
  recovery path.
- Prepared records, deterministic presentation, and optional offline extraction are
  described distinctly.

For the smallest real-user experiment, proposed thresholds are not results: continue
if at least two of three German-capable Berlin residents reach the supported
conclusion without coaching within two minutes and name a concrete advantage over
the official page. Reconsider the map-first proposition if two cannot find a record,
prefer the official page without lost context, or interpret a plan as completion.

## Dependencies

No credential is needed for the public reading journey. A publishable build requires
`PUBLICATION_AS_OF_DATE` and the existing owner-approved `LEGAL_ADDRESS`. A future
authorized extraction run requires `OPENAI_API_KEY`, a retained document, database
identifiers, an explicit call count, and a cost ceiling; it is not part of this
product test.

## The Assignment

Recruit three German-capable Berlin residents with a real construction question.
Alternate official-page-first and atlas-first order. Observe them without coaching,
then record whether they can explain the date, source boundary, and remaining
unknowns, plus whether the history provided a reason to use the atlas again.
