# Category coverage map

These generic categories are the coverage map that justified the four scouting lanes. They predate the named longlist and are retained so lane selection stays auditable; they contain no named candidate and no C-0NN ID.

## Transport and infrastructure

| Candidate lead | Why it is in the pool | Source availability | Expected end-date evidence | Budget / procurement signal | Geographic usefulness | Risks | Next verification step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tram extension or new-build corridor, such as an Ostkreuz-area alignment | Tests route-scale works, phasing and public disruption | Planfeststellung and BVG/Senate pages are plausible; exact project boundary unverified | Likely milestone trail; exact current date unknown | Plan documents, notices and budget records likely; measure type unknown | Very high: route, stops and affected districts | Multiple phases/lots; planned versus delivered opening can blur | Match the exact corridor to current official planfeststellungs and BVG records; extract German milestone terms |
| U-Bahn works or operating-facility expansion, e.g. Britz-Süd or Friedrichsfelde | Tests formal approval, operator evidence and long time horizons | Official planfeststellung entry points exist; candidate identity and scope require matching | Possibly an official construction/commissioning date; unknown | Procurement and Senate investment signals plausible; no amount verified | High: network and neighbourhood impact | Facility construction may be less visible; approvals are technical PDFs | Verify whether the lead is a project or a subproject; find current expected completion and approval documents |
| Bridge, tunnel or major road rehabilitation | Tests infrastructure where public opening, construction start and completion differ | Senate/district construction pages and procurement notices plausible | Often reported as traffic-reopening date; exact evidence unknown | VOB notices and budget/investment programme likely | High: precise disruption footprint | Maintenance may be too small or bundled; contract lot may not equal project | Select one bounded work only after finding an official project page and a dated end/reopening source |
| S-Bahn, rail or station accessibility works | Tests operator, federal/state boundary and accessibility milestones | DB/S-Bahn/BVG plus Berlin notices likely; authority mix unverified | Often a service-reopening date, but construction end type unknown | TED/Berlin procurement may be incomplete; funding split risk | High and resident-facing | Federal infrastructure may sit outside Berlin records; names repeat | Establish commissioning authority and search official operator, plan and procurement records |

| School new build or major school renovation | Directly useful; tests district budgets, Schulbauoffensive and occupied-site phases | District, Senate school-building and budget sources plausible | Usually a completion or handover target; candidate-specific evidence unknown | Investment programme and construction procurement likely | High: schools are addressable neighbourhood anchors | Programme name may hide individual projects; school name can change | Pick a concrete school only after matching project ID/address across district and Senate sources |
| Hospital or public-health facility construction | Tests complex commissioning and institutional responsibility | Public owner/operator and planning sources plausible; access varies | Commissioning/opening may be public; exact construction end unknown | Budget, operator and procurement signals likely but may be private/public mixed | High for local service users | Legal/privacy and private operator boundaries; multiple buildings | Verify public role, project boundary, and whether sources support a construction story rather than an operating expansion |
| Sports hall, swimming pool or sports-campus renewal | Tests smaller district works and useful local geography | District pages and investment plans plausible; exact source trail unknown | Handover/reopening likely but not verified | District investment programme and VOB notices plausible | Medium-high | May be too small, bundled or paused; sparse updates | Find an official project page, address and supported reopening/completion milestone |
| Museum, theatre or cultural-building renovation | Tests heritage constraints, phased reopening and public communication | Senate/district/cultural institution pages plausible | Reopening date may be prominent; construction end type unknown | Budget, grants and procurement may be split | High public usefulness and storytelling value | Famous-project bias; heritage permissions and private partners | Verify official scope, funding measure types and whether the candidate adds a distinct data problem |

| Drinking-water, sewer or district-energy works | Tests utility/operator sources and buried infrastructure | Berliner Wasserbetriebe / energy operator notices plausible; exact source coverage unknown | Reopening or phase dates may exist; construction end unknown | Operator procurement and public works notices may be partial | High for disruption and service geography | Private/regulated operator boundaries; works may be maintenance rather than a project | Verify public-facing project identity, responsible role and a date source; do not infer cost from a tender |

## Road closures and lane restrictions

| Candidate lead | Why it is in the pool | Source availability | Expected end-date evidence | Budget / procurement signal | Geographic usefulness | Risks | Next verification step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Road, bridge or tunnel work closing at least one lane | Directly affects traffic and tests the resident-facing value of current status and end date | Senate mobility, district, traffic-management and operator notices are plausible; exact closure lead unverified | Closure end/reopening dates may be published, but construction completion may be different; unknown | Works notices and procurement may be separate; do not infer project cost from a closure notice | Very high for route users and nearby residents | Temporary traffic order may not identify the underlying project; dates can be repeatedly extended | Match the closure to an official work/project identity, then verify the closure period and construction milestone separately |
| Utility or emergency works causing sustained lane loss | Captures works that appear suddenly and may have sparse public explanation | Utility, district, traffic and police/authority notices may be fragmented | Often a reopening estimate rather than a project end date; unknown | Procurement may be unavailable or unrelated to the urgent work | High locally, especially on constrained routes | Emergency work, private utility and construction work can be conflated | Identify the responsible organization and legal traffic notice; record as observed/lead until project identity is supported |
| Major junction, cycleway or bus-priority redesign with lane changes | Tests planned transport change and competing user impacts | Senate/district project pages, participation records and works notices plausible | Planned completion/reopening likely but not verified | Public works procurement and investment records may exist | High: route and neighbourhood effects are mappable | Planning proposal may be mistaken for started construction; phase boundaries may shift | Verify construction start and current expected end date from an official project source |

Road closures are not automatically separate projects. Where a closure is a
symptom of a larger bounded work, link it to that project and preserve the
closure as a dated disruption event. Where no underlying project can yet be
identified, keep it in the backlog rather than inventing one.
