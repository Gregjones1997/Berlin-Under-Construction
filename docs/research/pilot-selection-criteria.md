# Pilot-selection criteria

**Status:** Accepted research policy; difficulty rule amended by the project owner 2026-08-06
**Phase:** Phase 1 — research foundation
**Last updated:** 2026-08-07

This document freezes the gates and provisional scoring model for choosing the
three first-release pilot projects. It does not select any project. Candidate
names and source references in the companion ledger remain leads until the
project owner verifies the original German sources.

## Purpose

The pilots must test the product's evidence path, not merely produce three
recognisable map pins. Together they should cover different source families,
document problems, geographic contexts and degrees of research difficulty while
still being useful to a resident trying to understand a real construction story.

The selection unit is a **project story**, not an isolated tender, building
permit, contract lot or news event. A candidate may contain several linked lots
or phases, but the boundary must be written down before scoring.

**Research difficulty is recorded after an evidence pass, never estimated from
the identity source.** Discovery-stage difficulty ratings are provisional and
must not drive portfolio selection as if they were observed facts. Identity
pages routinely conceal parliamentary PDFs, access barriers, dead links and
cross-source contradictions; C-010 and C-014 demonstrated this directly.

## Hard eligibility gates

A candidate is eligible for scoring only if all gates below are provisionally
met. “Provisional” means a lead is sufficient to enter the ledger; it is not a
publication decision.

1. **Berlin relevance:** the work is physically in Berlin or materially changes
   Berlin infrastructure, public space or public service.
2. **Construction substance:** it is construction, reconstruction, conversion,
   major rehabilitation or a closely related infrastructure work. A routine
   service, policy, event or purely commercial announcement is not enough.
3. **Project identity lead:** at least one plausible official or owner/operator
   source identifies the work, even if the name, boundary or responsible body is
   not yet resolved.
4. **Evidence recoverability:** at least one lawful public evidence path exists
   to investigate the candidate. A search result alone is not evidence of a
   claim.
5. **User-facing consequence:** the work affects a public place, public
   service, housing supply, mobility, utilities, safety, environmental quality
   or a substantial number of residents.
6. **Research boundary:** the candidate can be bounded to a project or phase
   without silently combining unrelated contracts or developments.
7. **No automatic exclusion for difficulty:** inaccessible, scanned, moved or
   fragmented sources do not fail a candidate by themselves. They are recorded
   as research risk and may lower the score.

An observed work with no identifiable end date can still enter the research
backlog. It scores exactly 0 for the expected-end-date dimension and cannot
pass the final-selection gate until the date question is resolved. This keeps
an important resident question visible without treating an undated work as
pilot-ready.

The following are **final-selection gates**, applied after research rather than
used to erase the candidate pool:

- The project has a current status and an **expected end date** (or a directly
  supported statement that no expected end date is publicly published) from an
  authoritative source, with the exact German wording retained.
- Material identity, location, responsible role(s), dates and financial claims
  selected for the pilot have evidence spans.
- The project can be presented without naming natural persons and without
  implying unsupported responsibility for delay or cost variance.
- Source use, quoting, retention and links pass the project's legal/licensing
  review before publication.

## Weighted score: 0–100

Score each dimension from 0 to 5, then multiply by its weight divided by five.
Use whole or half points only, and record a short reason and source status for
every score. A score is a research aid, not a model-generated truth claim.

| Dimension | Weight | What a high score means |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Expected end-date evidence and user clarity | **25** | A current, project-level expected end date is explicit, typed correctly, dated, and comparable with earlier supported dates. |
| Public usefulness and resident consequence | 20 | The project answers an important practical question for many people, not just an industry or political audience. |
| Evidence coverage and source diversity | 20 | Identity, location, dates, money, responsibility and change history can be checked across relevant authoritative sources. |
| Change and accountability value | 15 | The story can show meaningful promises, milestones, revisions, procurement or oversight without inferring blame. |
| Geographic usefulness | 10 | The project is mappable at a useful public scale and helps explain an area, route, district or citywide pattern. |
| Learning value and portfolio fit | 10 | It tests a distinct document, category, lifecycle or extraction problem and complements the other pilots. |
| **Total** | **100** | |

### Scoring rules

- A missing expected end date is not scored as neutral: it is **exactly 0** for
  the end-date dimension. A clearly published “date not yet determined” can
  score above 0 only when the wording and publication date are directly
  supported; it does not become an invented date.
- An expected end date is not interchangeable with `Baubeginn`, `Bauzeit`,
  `Inbetriebnahme`, `Verkehrsfreigabe`, handover or a contract deadline. Store
  the German milestone type first.
- A project with a high source score but little public consequence should not
  outrank a useful project solely because it is easier to research.
- A difficult project can score well on usefulness and learning value while
  scoring lower on evidence coverage. It remains in the pool for a second
  verification pass.
- Do not add unverified numbers, dates, contractors or organizations to improve
  a score. “Unknown” is a valid value.

## Bias controls

### Source-availability bias

Well-indexed, HTML-rich projects are easier to score than projects documented
in scanned PDFs, district archives, procurement systems or changing operator
pages. Source availability is therefore only one dimension, and it is paired
with a retained “research difficulty” note. The ledger must keep hard-to-find
projects, failed search paths, dead links and unresolved gaps. At least one
shortlisted project should test fragmented or difficult evidence, subject to
the final evidence gate and available review time.

The retained research-difficulty note is finalized only after the evidence pass.
Any rating recorded during discovery must be labelled provisional and replaced
or confirmed from the actual retrieval trail before this control is assessed.

### Famous-project bias

Large, heavily covered projects can dominate discovery because search engines
and press releases repeat their names. Search and ledger review must include
district-level works, public buildings, utilities, housing and less famous
projects. Popularity is not a score dimension. A famous project must earn its
place through public usefulness and evidence value.

### Public-usefulness bias

Research convenience and institutional prominence are not substitutes for
resident value. “Affected people” is not a requirement to estimate an exact
headcount. Score usefulness using the severity and duration of practical
disruption, service change, spatial reach, cost significance, affected-place
context and the ability to answer “what is happening here and when will it
end?”. A project can be highly useful to a smaller neighbourhood even when its
citywide population is low. Record who is affected and why rather than reducing
that judgment to a falsely precise number. Do not equate expensive with useful,
central with important, or official visibility with public value.

### Portfolio and selection leakage

The rubric is frozen before finalist scoring. Research agents may discover and
describe candidates, but they do not select the final three or create the
golden truth set. Their output enters a human-owned research backlog with a
discovery source, candidate status, unresolved questions and next verification
step. The final portfolio should normally include one bounded case,
one complex multi-source case and one difficult-to-discover case, unless direct
verification shows that this would weaken the release.

## Selection record to complete later

For every scored candidate, retain:

- project boundary, aliases and category;
- each dimension score and the evidence status behind it;
- expected end-date wording, milestone type, source date and as-of date;
- source-availability and research-difficulty notes separately from usefulness;
- disqualifying or unresolved gates;
- final human decision, rationale and verification links.

For every undated or poorly identified work in the backlog, also retain:

- how it was observed or discovered, including date and location;
- the current status (`lead`, `searching`, `blocked`, `verified`, or `closed`);
- the exact end-date question still open; and
- the next source family or manual check to try.

No checklist item should be marked complete until this record exists and the
three selected projects have manually verified dossiers.
